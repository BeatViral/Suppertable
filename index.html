Safety & compliance

What your kitchen brand needs before opening.

SupperTable helps turn a talented cook into a properly set-up local food operator. A kitchen brand does not open on SupperTable until the required setup has been reviewed.

Identity check Food Safety Supervisor pathway Council registration pathway Home business steps if needed Kitchen information Menu and allergen information Pickup and delivery process Manual review before activation Incident reporting

No kitchen brand can open on SupperTable until the required setup has been reviewed.

Kitchen owners are independent food operators responsible for their own food preparation. SupperTable provides the storefront, checklist, order tools and document workflow.

Kitchen checklist

The setup pathway before a kitchen brand opens.

This pilot checklist is based on the Northern Rivers and Ballina Shire pathway. It may change by council area, property type and menu risk. It is setup guidance, not legal advice.

1

Food Safety Supervisor pathway

Complete the training pathway that applies to the kitchen activity and upload evidence.

2

Council food business steps

Follow the local council pathway before selling food through SupperTable.

3

Home business check

If food is prepared at home, confirm the required acknowledgement, permission or planning step.

4

Kitchen and menu review

Upload kitchen photos, ingredients, allergens, batch limits, pickup windows and delivery process.

Kitchen owner ID and contact details ABN or business details if available Food Safety Supervisor evidence Food premises or local council evidence Home business acknowledgement if required Kitchen photos: fridge, sink, bench, stove and storage Menu, ingredients and allergen list Pickup and local delivery process Insurance pathway acknowledgement

A simple pilot sequence.

Collect documents, complete the required food-safety steps, confirm the local council pathway, prepare the menu and fulfilment details, then submit the kitchen packet for SupperTable review.

FSS training

Provider course costs vary. The NSW Food Authority says RTOs may only charge $30 to issue the NSW FSS certificate, which must be renewed every 5 years.

Important: local pathways can change.

The first pilot is Northern Rivers focused. Other councils may use different forms, fees, inspections and approval steps. SupperTable should only activate kitchens where the local pathway is clear enough to review properly.
